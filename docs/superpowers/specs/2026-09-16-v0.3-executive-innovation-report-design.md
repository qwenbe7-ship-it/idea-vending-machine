# Idea Vending Machine v0.3 — Executive Innovation Report Design

## 1. Purpose

Idea Vending Machine v0.3 changes the product from a raw-idea structuring tool into an evidence-grounded innovation decision system for founders, CEOs, business owners, product leaders, innovation teams, and investors.

The system must not merely elaborate the user's first idea. It must determine whether the idea deserves investment, identify the underlying problem and obsolete assumptions, research the current market, generate materially different evolved concepts, test those concepts against evidence and counter-evidence, and present an executive decision report before any specification or implementation work begins.

The guiding principle is:

> Do not build the first idea. Discover what the idea should become, prove why, and only then decide whether to build it.

The v0.2 deterministic Agent MD analyzer remains valuable, but moves downstream as a verification and feasibility gate rather than serving as the main creative intelligence.

---

## 2. Product Outcome

For each idea, the system produces two layers of output.

### Layer A — Executive Decision Brief

This must fit the first screen or first 1–2 report pages and allow a decision-maker to understand the conclusion in approximately three minutes.

Required fields:

1. **Decision** — `GO`, `MODIFY`, `HOLD`, or `KILL`.
2. **One-sentence thesis** — the single most important conclusion.
3. **Why now** — the market/technology timing argument.
4. **What changes from the original idea** — short Evolution Delta.
5. **Market snapshot** — current market, forecast market, CAGR/range, stage.
6. **Best customer / buyer** — who experiences measurable economic value.
7. **Business value** — revenue creation, cost reduction, risk reduction, or capital avoidance.
8. **Top three reasons to pursue**.
9. **Top three reasons not to pursue**.
10. **Critical unknowns** — facts that remain unproven.
11. **Cheapest next validation** — the next action before significant investment.

The executive layer must never hide important uncertainty. If a conclusion depends on weak evidence, the uncertainty must be visible here rather than buried in the appendix.

### Layer B — Evidence & Analysis Report

The remainder of the report explains and substantiates Layer A in depth. It contains market research, sources, methodology, assumptions, competition, counter-evidence, evolved concepts, feasibility, economics, risks, and validation design.

The product is considered successful only when the executive layer is traceable to the detailed evidence layer.

---

## 3. Report Layout

The canonical report structure is fixed as follows.

### Part I — Executive Summary

#### 1. Decision Card
- Verdict: GO / MODIFY / HOLD / KILL
- Confidence: high / medium / low
- One-sentence thesis
- Investment posture: build now / validate first / revisit later / stop

#### 2. Key Numbers
A compact table with:
- current relevant market size
- forecast market size
- CAGR or forecast range
- current market stage
- AI/technology adoption signal
- estimated buyer type
- estimated initial validation cost or effort when available

Each market number must include source, source date, base year, forecast year, and definition.

#### 3. Why This Matters
Three to five concise bullets explaining the opportunity or the reason to stop.

#### 4. Evolution Delta
A side-by-side view:

| Original Idea | Evolved Opportunity |
|---|---|
| original problem framing | revised problem framing |
| original customer | higher-value buyer |
| original workflow | redesigned workflow |
| original value | new measurable value |
| original business model | stronger value capture |

#### 5. Reasons For / Reasons Against
A balanced two-column summary. The report must not be allowed to show only supportive evidence.

#### 6. CEO Action
One concrete next step with a measurable pass/fail condition.

---

### Part II — Strategic Analysis

#### 7. Original Idea & Intent
Preserve the user's original wording, then infer the intended outcome without silently replacing it.

#### 8. Underlying Problem
Separate:
- visible symptom
- user pain
- economic loss
- root cause
- cost of inaction

#### 9. Market Definition
Define the market narrowly enough to be decision-useful. Avoid claiming the entire generative-AI market as TAM when only a small subsegment is relevant.

The report may show multiple nested markets:
- adjacent platform market
- direct software category
- emerging AI-enabled subcategory
- realistically addressable initial segment

#### 10. Global Market Status
Explain:
- market maturity
- adoption stage
- current buyers
- dominant use cases
- capital/investment direction
- regulation/technology shifts
- likely 2–5 year transition

Market stage vocabulary:
- research/emergence
- category formation
- early adoption
- early scale
- mainstream growth
- mature/consolidating
- declining/replaced

#### 11. Market Size & Forecast
For each material market estimate record:
- publisher
- publication/update date
- base year
- base value
- forecast year
- forecast value
- CAGR
- geographic scope
- included/excluded segments
- confidence tier

Never average incompatible market reports into a fake precise figure. When definitions conflict, report a range and explain why.

#### 12. Competitive Landscape
Include:
- direct competitors
- adjacent competitors
- substitutes
- generic AI alternatives
- internal/manual workaround

For each competitor compare the customer problem solved, workflow position, economic model, strengths, weaknesses, and what the proposed concept changes structurally rather than cosmetically.

#### 13. Customer Economics
Estimate or qualitatively classify:
- current labor/time cost
- error/risk cost
- revenue opportunity
- switching cost
- willingness-to-pay basis
- buyer vs user

Any numerical estimate without reliable external data must be labeled as an assumption or scenario, not a fact.

---

### Part III — Idea Evolution

#### 14. Assumption Map
List the important assumptions embedded in the existing workflow or market.

Examples:
- a human must review this
- the customer must initiate the process
- the document is the primary unit of work
- the service occurs after the event
- the product must be purchased as software

#### 15. Assumption Destruction
Systematically test transformations such as:
- AFTER → BEFORE
- REPAIR → PREVENT
- SEARCH → PREDICT
- ADVISE → EXECUTE
- INPUT → OBSERVE
- TOOL → WORKFLOW
- WORKFLOW → INFRASTRUCTURE
- DOCUMENT → DATA
- DATA → DECISION
- DECISION → ACTION
- SERVICE → ASSET
- ONE-TIME VALUE → COMPOUNDING VALUE

The system does not have to use every transformation, but it must record which transformations were tested and which materially changed the opportunity.

#### 16. Cross-Industry Mechanism Transfer
Do not borrow brand names or superficial features. Extract reusable mechanisms from other industries.

Each transfer must state:
1. source industry/mechanism,
2. why the mechanism works there,
3. what equivalent constraint exists in the target problem,
4. how the mechanism changes the target value chain,
5. expected new customer value,
6. new risks introduced.

#### 17. Candidate Forge
Generate materially different concept families, not minor feature variations.

Minimum families:
- **Adjacent Innovation** — dramatically improve the original solution.
- **Category Shift** — redefine what product/category solves the problem.
- **Zero-Based Reinvention** — redesign the workflow as if today's technology existed from the beginning.
- **Axion Candidate** — maximize automation, capital efficiency, repeatability, delivery stability, and margin while avoiding prohibited SaaS-first assumptions.

#### 18. Candidate Critique
A separate evaluator context attacks each candidate for:
- weak problem evidence
- low willingness to pay
- incumbent response
- copyability
- operational burden
- regulatory exposure
- AI reliability risk
- data dependency
- distribution weakness
- cost/complexity mismatch

The generator must not grade its own output alone.

#### 19. Evolution Delta
For surviving candidates, quantify or describe what improved relative to the raw idea:
- problem depth
- customer value
- buyer value
- automation level
- feasibility
- revenue model
- defensibility
- compounding/data effects
- distribution potential

If no meaningful improvement is found, the system must explicitly say that the original idea should remain unchanged or be killed.

---

### Part IV — Decision & Execution

#### 20. Evidence For
Strongest evidence supporting investment.

#### 21. Evidence Against
Strongest evidence contradicting the opportunity. Research must actively search for failure cases, substitutes, unfavorable economics, regulation, and signs of weak customer demand.

#### 22. Agent MD Gate
Evaluate:
- automation level A0–A5
- implementation feasibility T1–T5
- AI judgment vs deterministic-rule boundary
- exception handling
- human approval requirements
- verification strategy
- production reliability risk

#### 23. Risk Register
At minimum:
- market risk
- technology risk
- model reliability risk
- data risk
- regulation/legal risk
- security/privacy risk
- operational risk
- distribution risk
- business-model risk
- competitive risk

Each material risk gets probability/impact qualitative level, mitigation, and kill trigger where applicable.

#### 24. What Must Be True
List the smallest set of assumptions that must hold for the project to succeed.

Each item must be falsifiable where practical.

#### 25. Validation Plan
Design the cheapest tests first.

For each assumption:
- hypothesis
- experiment
- evidence threshold
- cost/time appetite
- pass condition
- fail condition
- decision if failed

#### 26. Final Decision
Allowed verdicts:
- **GO** — evidence supports moving into specification.
- **MODIFY** — opportunity exists but the evolved concept should replace the raw idea.
- **HOLD** — potentially attractive but blocked by missing evidence/timing/dependency.
- **KILL** — expected value is insufficient relative to cost/risk or a clearly superior alternative exists.

A `GO` or `MODIFY` result may hand off to v0.2 development-package generation. `HOLD` and `KILL` must not silently proceed to specification.

## 4. Evidence Architecture

### 4.1 Evidence Record

Every external claim that materially affects the decision is represented as an evidence record:

```text
claim_id
claim
source_title
source_url
publisher
publication_date
retrieved_at
geography
population_or_market_definition
evidence_type
supports_or_contradicts
confidence_tier
freshness_status
candidate_ids
notes
```

### 4.2 Confidence Tiers

- **Tier A** — primary/official source, regulator, audited filing, peer-reviewed/high-quality academic source, first-party authoritative product data.
- **Tier B** — reputable strategy/research institution or transparent market-research methodology.
- **Tier C** — credible secondary reporting, trade publication, expert analysis.
- **Tier D** — anecdotal/community/uncorroborated material; may suggest a hypothesis but cannot independently justify a GO decision.

Material market-size and regulatory claims should prefer Tier A/B and be cross-checked when feasible.

### 4.3 Market-Size Integrity Rules

1. Never present one vendor's forecast as unquestionable truth.
2. Never combine markets with different definitions without explaining the mismatch.
3. Show forecast date and base date explicitly.
4. Label estimates, scenarios, and derived calculations.
5. Distinguish TAM, SAM, SOM, adjacent market, and enabling technology market.
6. Prefer ranges when source definitions materially differ.
7. Flag paywalled/secondary citations separately from directly verified primary material.

## 5. Expert-Agent Responsibilities

The v0.3 architecture separates generation and evaluation responsibilities.

### Research roles
- Market Researcher
- Competitive Intelligence Analyst
- Technology Scout
- Regulatory/Risk Researcher

### Reframing roles
- First-Principles Analyst
- Customer Economics Analyst
- Contrarian Strategist
- Cross-Industry Mechanism Analyst

### Generation roles
- Innovation Architect
- Business Model Architect
- Automation Architect

### Evaluation roles
- Independent Investment Judge
- Feasibility Engineer
- Risk/Red-Team Evaluator
- Evidence Auditor

The final synthesis agent may use all artifacts, but evaluators must receive the candidates as objects to critique rather than inheriting the generator's persuasive chain-of-thought or self-justification.

## 6. State Machine

Canonical v0.3 flow:

```text
RAW IDEA
  ↓
INTAKE
  ↓
RESEARCH PASS 1
  ↓
PROBLEM / INTENT DEFINITION
  ↓
ASSUMPTION MAP
  ↓
REFRAME + CROSS-INDUSTRY TRANSFER
  ↓
CANDIDATE FORGE
  ↓
RESEARCH PASS 2 / COLLISION CHECK
  ↓
INDEPENDENT CRITIQUE
  ↓
EVIDENCE + AGENT MD GATE
  ↓
EXECUTIVE INNOVATION REPORT
  ↓
HUMAN DECISION
  ├─ GO/MODIFY → SPEC → DESIGN → PLAN
  ├─ HOLD → VALIDATION / RESEARCH LOOP
  └─ KILL → ARCHIVE WITH REASON
```

A report generation is not complete until all required artifacts are present and the Evidence Auditor confirms no material unsupported claim is presented as fact.

## 7. Research Strategy

Research runs in two distinct passes.

### Pass 1 — Landscape Research
Purpose: understand reality without anchoring the generator too heavily on incumbent solutions.

Collect:
- problem evidence
- customer workflow
- market size/stage
- adoption signals
- relevant technology shifts
- relevant regulation
- broad competitor map
- evidence against the premise

### Pass 2 — Collision Research
Purpose: test the generated ideas against the world.

Collect:
- direct prior art
- similar products
- failed attempts
- patents where relevant
- price and business model comparisons
- dependency constraints
- regulatory blockers
- actual buyer evidence

The second pass may invalidate a candidate and must be allowed to do so.

## 8. User Experience

The first screen after analysis must not display a long report wall.

Recommended interaction:

### Screen 1 — Decision Brief
- verdict badge
- one-sentence thesis
- four key market numbers
- evolved idea summary
- three reasons for
- three reasons against
- critical unknown
- recommended next validation

Buttons:
- `상세 근거 보기`
- `다른 진화안 비교`
- `검증 계획 보기`
- `이 방향 승인`

### Screen 2 — Evidence Report
Expandable sections matching Parts II–IV.

### Screen 3 — Decision
User explicitly selects:
- proceed with evolved concept
- choose alternative candidate
- request refinement
- hold
- kill

Only explicit GO/MODIFY approval enables development-package generation.

## 9. Quality Gates

v0.3 requires both software tests and AI-quality evaluations.

### Deterministic gates
- report schema validation
- required section presence
- source metadata completeness
- no unsupported market-size claim marked as fact
- no GO without problem/evidence/concept artifacts
- no spec handoff after HOLD/KILL
- citations resolve to stored evidence records
- original idea preserved verbatim
- generator/evaluator role separation preserved

### AI/evaluation gates
Benchmark set must include:
- weak ideas
- obviously crowded ideas
- highly regulated ideas
- ideas with false market premises
- technically impossible ideas
- strong ideas that should not be over-modified
- ideas where the best outcome is KILL
- ideas where the best outcome is HOLD
- cross-industry opportunities

Metrics:
- evidence coverage
- counter-evidence coverage
- unsupported-claim rate
- meaningful-reframe rate
- false-GO rate
- evaluator agreement/disagreement rate
- user-selected evolution rate
- report usefulness rating
- later validation/build outcome where available

`NO GREEN WITHOUT EVIDENCE` remains mandatory.

---

## 10. Persistence & Auditability

Each assessment receives an `evolution_id` and stores:
- raw idea
- normalized intent
- research queries
- evidence records
- assumptions
- transformations attempted
- candidate concepts
- critiques
- scores/ratings
- rejected concepts and reasons
- final verdict
- user decision
- downstream spec/design/plan if created
- model/provider/version metadata
- generation timestamps
- cost/usage metadata when available

The history is an audit trail and later becomes an outcome-learning dataset. Raw research content is treated as untrusted data, not executable instructions.

---

## 11. Current Market Rationale for v0.3

The architecture is aligned with a real transition in the market rather than only internal preference.

As of 2026:
- enterprise AI adoption is broad, but full-scale value creation remains uncommon;
- agentic AI is in early scale/category-formation, with rapid growth but high project-cancellation risk;
- innovation platforms are adding AI-powered research and decision support;
- current spec-driven engineering tools increasingly separate idea assessment from implementation.

Therefore the product should compete on **decision quality, evidence quality, reframing quality, and validated handoff**, not on generic text generation.

## 12. Scope for v0.3

### In scope
- executive-summary-first report schema
- evidence model
- two-pass research contract
- expert-role contracts
- assumption/reframing framework
- candidate generation contract
- independent evaluator contract
- Agent MD handoff gate
- GO/MODIFY/HOLD/KILL state model
- validation-plan generation
- UI flow for summary → evidence → decision
- quality benchmark design

### Out of scope for this design
- autonomous deployment
- billing/subscriptions
- user authentication
- direct investment execution
- autonomous legal/financial decisions
- claiming guaranteed commercial success
- replacing professional regulated advice

Each PR follows RED → GREEN → review → exact-head squash merge → fresh main Gate.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria for the v0.3 Design

The v0.3 implementation is not complete until all of the following are demonstrably true:

1. A user can understand the core recommendation without reading the full report.
2. Every material recommendation can be traced to evidence, assumptions, or explicit inference.
3. Market-size claims state source, date, definition, and forecast horizon.
4. Supporting and contradicting evidence are both present for nontrivial decisions.
5. At least three materially different concept families can be produced when the idea merits reframing.
6. The system can honestly return HOLD or KILL.
7. Generator and independent evaluator responsibilities are separated.
8. The original idea and the evolved idea are shown side-by-side.
9. GO/MODIFY decisions produce a concrete validation step before substantial investment.
10. Only an explicitly approved GO/MODIFY concept can proceed to v0.2 spec/design/plan generation.
11. All deterministic gates and benchmark evals pass before release.
12. No success claim is made without fresh verification evidence.
